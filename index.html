Vessel AIS tracking updated nightly at 11pm. Track color is an absolute speed scale — the same knots mean the same color on every vessel and every day, so tracks are comparable across the fleet. It is non-linear on purpose: most of the ramp covers the 0.5–5 kt fishing band (the legend tick marks 5 kt), while 8 kt and up is compressed, so a day of steady steaming reads as close to one color. The day's own speed range is in the status line above; 🐟 markers flag a dwell of at least 15 continuous minutes at 0.5–5 kt outside excluded harbor/base zones (dashed outline) as a fishing-likely heuristic, not a confirmed catch. A grey dashed chord marks a reporting gap over 10 minutes — real position and speed are unknown for that stretch, so the marker holds at the last confirmed fix instead of animating across it, and the readout shows “signal gap.” Drag the handle (or press play) to step the marker along the day’s reports; playback speed is a multiple of real elapsed time, so 500× replays the whole day in 1/500th of it. On a touch screen one finger scrolls the page — hold with two fingers to pan the map.

Validated for every boat

Weather-first boat forecasts

Every boat is forecast with a regularized weather model using observed sea-surface temperature, swell, AM/PM, and the boat’s recent encounter state. It contains no date, month, day-of-year, or seasonal predictor.

Validation is chronological: models learn from 2024, then face unseen 2025–2026 trips. Boats with enough history receive their own fitted model. Sparse-history boats use the shared fleet model, which is tested on the same unseen period; they are no longer untested Dolphin transfers.

Holdout: later trips set aside for the closed-book test; they do not teach the validation model.

MAE (average miss): how far a forecast is from the actual result on average, measured in fish per angler. Lower is better.

RMSE (large-miss score): another error measure that penalizes big misses more heavily. Lower is better.

Within ±2 fish: the share of forecasts that landed within 2 fish per angler of the actual result. Higher is better.

Range calibration: signed actual-minus-forecast errors create asymmetric 50% and 80% ranges. Boat-specific residuals are used when sufficient; sparse boats use the validated fleet residual distribution. The 80% band captured of holdout outcomes.

Training + weighting

How the forecast learns

Chronological validation: a test copy of the model learns only from 2018–2024 trips. It then forecasts 2025–2026 trips that were kept out of training, and those forecasts are compared with the actual fish per angler. This is a closed-book test: the model does not get to study the answers first. The final published fit uses all eligible history through the frozen data date.

Learned weights: water temperature, water-temperature curvature, swell, swell curvature, AM/PM, recent 12-trip catch level, the PM-by-water-temperature interaction, and signed tide change are scaled before fitting. Ridge regression (λ = 12) learns their coefficients from the trips and shrinks weak or noisy effects toward 0; no feature receives a hand-set percentage.

Boat policy: boats with at least 20 matched training trips receive their own fit. Boats with less history use the chronologically tested fleet model. Fish per angler is capped at 20 while fitting so isolated reports cannot dominate.

Current status: training data and model updates are frozen through August 30, 2026 for the 4-week prospective validation window. The forecast will not retrain until updates are explicitly resumed.

Freshness policy

How the model stays current

Run a scheduled refresh once daily after dock totals normally settle. The refresh should re-read the latest 14 report days (so late corrections are captured), merge records by a stable source-row fingerprint, refresh NOAA observations and the NWS 7-day grid, retrain every boat model, rerun the chronological holdout, and rebuild this static page.

Publishing guardrail: deploy only when the scraper succeeds, historical row counts do not unexpectedly shrink, forecast inputs are recent, and the fleet validation report is complete.

Icon treatment

The fish illustrations are original inline vector motifs inspired by the compact side-profile card style in the user’s reference image. They do not reuse the copyrighted FishDatabase artwork shown on the source site. Barracuda, Bluefin, Bonito, Calico Bass, Dorado, Sand Bass, Skipjack, Yellowfin, and Yellowtail receive species-specific color/body treatments; every other species uses a generic fish mark.
